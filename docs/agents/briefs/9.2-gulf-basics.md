# 9.2 Gulf basics: work-permit notes and reserved professions

## Goal
A job seeker looking at a role in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain or Oman sees short, dated notes on how work
permits work there, and is warned when the role's profession is reserved for nationals in that country. Every statement links
an official source and its date, so a wrong or outdated note can be spotted and fixed.

## Prior art to check first
career-ops' Arabic modes (Arab labour-law terms such as end-of-service gratuity and allowances): reuse their terms and
check whether they already cite Gulf sources. If career-ops would take this data, propose it there too (Track A).

## What to build
1. **First PR: sources only, no import.** Research and propose, per country, the official sources: the ministry or authority,
   the page or decision, its date, the format (HTML, PDF, open-data file), the licence or terms of use, and whether automated
   download is allowed. Candidates to verify, not facts: UAE MOHRE and the u.ae portal; Saudi HRSD (Saudization decisions)
   and Qiwa; Qatar Ministry of Labour; Kuwait Public Authority for Manpower; Bahrain LMRA; Oman Ministry of Labour
   (Omanisation decisions). Write it to `docs/visa-sources/gulf.md` and stop for the owner's review.
2. Only official government sources, each with a publication or retrieval date. No news sites, recruiters, law-firm blogs or
   job boards. Never scrape a site whose terms forbid automated access; when a source can't be fetched, the data is entered
   by hand from it with the URL and date, and refreshed by hand.
3. After the sources are approved: one module per country (`src/lib/visa/ae.ts` …) implementing `VisaModule` from 9.1 with
   `notes` (each `{ text, sourceUrl, sourceDate }`) and `checkEmployer` returning `no_data` (no employer-level list exists).
4. `src/lib/visa/gulf/reservedProfessions.ts` (new): per country, the reserved professions as the decision words them, with
   the decision number, date, source URL, and the sectors or regions it applies to. A pure function
   `reservedFor(country, title)` matches a job title conservatively (exact or listed synonyms, English and Arabic as
   published) and returns the matching rule or null; no AI and no fuzzy guessing. A match is shown as "may be reserved for
   nationals: check the source", never as a verdict.
5. Data lives as typed TypeScript in the repo (small and reviewable); each country file carries `asOf` and a test fails when
   `asOf` is older than 12 months, prompting a manual review.
6. Keep wording neutral and country-agnostic in shared code; no legal advice. Notes in English and Arabic when the source
   publishes both (Arabic shown only after 10.4).

## Database
None: the data is static and versioned in git.

## Tests
`src/test/visa-gulf.test.ts`: every note and rule has a `https` source URL on a government domain and an ISO date; every
module satisfies `VisaModule`; `reservedFor` matches listed titles, ignores near-misses ("HR software engineer" is not
"HR specialist" unless listed), and returns null for other countries; the staleness check.
